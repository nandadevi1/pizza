For calibration, I'd use five test cases that exercise the calculator across **both flours, different flour quantities, and the temperature range**. I would deliberately include one warm case near the edge of the model.

> **UPDATE (2026-08-11):** The normal yeast baseline changed from **0.10%** to **1.0%** IDY, based on Tony Gemignani, *The Pizza Bible* (2014), "Master Dough without Starter" (4.5 g active dry yeast / 453 g flour = 1.0%). The 0.10% yeast values in the test cases below are superseded.

These use the current calculator assumptions:

* Oven: **220°C**
* Fridge: **7°C**
* Salt: **2.5%**
* Caputo 00 Pizzeria: **62% hydration**
* T65: **61% hydration**
* Normal yeast baseline: **0.10% IDY**
* At 29°C, the previous calculator adjustment of **0.08%** is used as an extrapolated warm-temperature starting point, not a validated value.
* Fermentation time is a **prediction/window**, with the dough's actual development overriding the clock.

### What your calculator should be checked against

For tests **1–4**, the important thing is that the calculator reproduces the percentages and scaling exactly:

* Water = flour × hydration
* Salt = flour × 2.5%
* Yeast = flour × 0.10%
* Total dough = sum of ingredients

For test **5**, it should recognize that **29°C is outside the validated temperature table** and label the yeast/time recommendation as an extrapolation rather than presenting false precision.

Also, the Caputo assumption is specifically **Pizzeria 00**, which Caputo currently specifies at 12.5% protein and W 260–280. ([Mulino Caputo][1]) Saf confirms Red is an instant yeast for lean/low-sugar doughs and pizza, and that it can be incorporated directly into the flour. ([Saf-instant®][2])

**One correction worth making to the markdown calculator:** I would change the yeast section so that **temperature changes fermentation time first**, while yeast percentage remains at 0.10% within the validated operating range. Only outside that range—such as 29°C—should the calculator optionally suggest a lower yeast dose and clearly label it as an extrapolation. That will make the calculator more scientifically defensible.
